Fourier Transformation

Mathematical Methods (207). Fourier series, arbitrary period, half-range expansions, Fourier integral, cosine and sine transforms, Fourier transform, DFT and FFT. Short forms are shown in bold brackets.

Overview

A periodic signal is a sum of sines and cosines (Fourier series, FS). Letting the period grow to infinity turns the sum into an integral (Fourier integral, FI), and this gives the Fourier transform (FT) for non-periodic functions. Sampling at \(N\) points gives the discrete Fourier transform (DFT), computed fast by the fast Fourier transform (FFT).

Road map. \(2\pi\)-periodic FS \(\to\) period \(2L\) FS \(\to\) \(L\to\infty\) FI \(\to\) cosine/sine transforms (even/odd) \(\to\) complex FT \(\to\) samples: DFT \(\to\) FFT. Laplace uses \(s=a+ib\) on \((0,\infty)\); Fourier uses \(i\omega\) on \((-\infty,\infty)\).

11.1 Fourier Series (FS)

Periodic functions and the series

\(f\) is periodic with period (T) \(2\pi\) if \(f(x+2\pi)=f(x)\) for all \(x\in\mathbb R\); frequency is \(1/T\). Its Fourier series is

\(f(x)=a_0+\sn\left(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx\right)\)

Euler formulas

\(\begin{gathered}a_0=\dfrac1{2\pi}\ip f\,dx\\[.8em]a_n=\dfrac1\pi\ip f\cos nx\,dx\\[.8em]b_n=\dfrac1\pi\ip f\sin nx\,dx\end{gathered}\)
Orthogonality (key insight). Over a full period \(\ip\cos nx\,dx=\ip\sin nx\,dx=0\) (equal positive and negative cycles); \(\ip\sin nx\cos mx\,dx=0\); \(\ip\cos nx\cos mx\,dx=\ip\sin nx\sin mx\,dx=0\) for \(n\ne m\) and \(=\pi\) for \(n=m\ge1\). This is why each coefficient can be isolated.

Example: square wave

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}-k,&-\pi\lt x\lt0\\k,&0\lt x\lt\pi\end{cases}\\[.8em]f(x+2\pi)=f(x)\end{gathered}\)

Step 1: symmetry

\(f\) is odd, so

\(\begin{gathered}a_0=0\\[.8em]a_n=0\end{gathered}\)

Step 2: find \(b_n\)

\(\begin{aligned}b_n&=\dfrac1\pi\Big[\int_{-\pi}^0(-k)\sin nx\,dx+\int_0^\pi k\sin nx\,dx\Big]\\[.8em]&=\dfrac{2k}{n\pi}\big(1-(-1)^n\big)\\[.8em]&=\begin{cases}\dfrac{4k}{n\pi},&n\text{ odd}\\0,&n\text{ even}\end{cases}\end{aligned}\)

Step 3: put \(n=2m+1\), \(m=0,1,\dots\)

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\dfrac{4k}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\sin(2m+1)x}{2m+1}\end{gathered}\)

Value at a jump

The series gives the average of the left and right limits:

\(\begin{aligned}f(0)&=\dfrac{f(0^-)+f(0^+)}2\\[.8em]&=\dfrac{-k+k}2\\[.8em]&=0\end{aligned}\)

Leibniz series

At \(x=\pi/2\), \(f=k\) and \(\sin\frac{(2m+1)\pi}2=(-1)^m\):

\(\begin{aligned}k&=\dfrac{4k}\pi\Big(1-\dfrac13+\dfrac15-\cdots\Big)\\[.8em]\dfrac\pi4&=1-\dfrac13+\dfrac15-\cdots\end{aligned}\)

11.2 Arbitrary Period, Even/Odd Functions, Half-Range Expansions

Arbitrary period \(2L\)

For \(f(x+2L)=f(x)\), the change of variable \(v=\pi x/L\) maps \([-L,L]\) to \([-\pi,\pi]\):

\(f(x)=a_0+\sn\left(a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\)
\(\begin{gathered}a_0=\dfrac1{2L}\iL f\,dx\\[.8em]a_n=\dfrac1L\iL f\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\[.8em]b_n=\dfrac1L\iL f\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\end{gathered}\)

Example: period-4 square wave

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}-4,&-2\lt x\lt0\\4,&0\lt x\lt2\end{cases}\\[.8em]f(x+4)=f(x)\end{gathered}\)

Here \(L=2\). \(f\) is odd, so \(a_0=a_n=0\):

\(\begin{aligned}b_n&=\dfrac22\int_0^24\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}2\,dx\\[.8em]&=\dfrac8{n\pi}(1-\cos n\pi)\\[.8em]&=\begin{cases}\dfrac{16}{n\pi},&n\text{ odd}\\0,&n\text{ even}\end{cases}\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\dfrac{4\times4}\pi\Big(\sin\dfrac{\pi x}2+\dfrac13\sin\dfrac{3\pi x}2+\dfrac15\sin\dfrac{5\pi x}2+\cdots\Big)\end{gathered}\)

Even and odd functions

Even \(f\) (Fourier cosine series): \(b_n=0\) and the interval is halved and doubled. Odd \(f\) (Fourier sine series): \(a_0=a_n=0\). Symmetry greatly reduces the integration.

Even \(f\) (Fourier cosine series):

\(\begin{gathered}b_n=0\\[.8em]a_0=\dfrac1L\int_0^Lf\,dx\\[.8em]a_n=\dfrac2L\int_0^Lf\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\end{gathered}\)

Odd \(f\) (Fourier sine series):

\(\begin{gathered}a_0=a_n=0\\[.8em]b_n=\dfrac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\end{gathered}\)

Linearity

The FS of a linear combination of functions is the same linear combination of their individual FS: \(\mathrm{FS}(\alpha f+\beta g)=\alpha\,\mathrm{FS}(f)+\beta\,\mathrm{FS}(g)\).

Example: \(f(x)=x+\pi\)

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=x+\pi,\quad-\pi\lt x\lt\pi\\[.8em]f(x+2\pi)=f(x)\end{gathered}\)

Let \(g(x)=x\). It is odd, so \(a_0=a_n=0\) and only \(b_n\) is needed:

\(\begin{aligned}b_n&=\dfrac1\pi\ip x\sin nx\,dx\\[.8em]&=-\dfrac2n\cos n\pi\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{gathered}g(x)=-2\sn\dfrac{\cos n\pi}n\sin nx\\[.8em]f(x)=g(x)+\pi=\pi-2\sn\dfrac{\cos n\pi}n\sin nx\end{gathered}\)

Half-range expansions (HRE)

A function defined only on \([0,L]\) is artificially extended to \([-L,L]\). Even periodic expansion \(f_e\): reflect symmetrically across the \(y\)-axis, giving a cosine series. Odd periodic expansion \(f_o\): reflect anti-symmetrically across the origin, giving a sine series.

Even periodic expansion:

\(\begin{gathered}f_e(x)=a_0+\sn a_n\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\\[.8em]a_0=\dfrac1L\int_0^Lf\,dx\\[.8em]a_n=\dfrac2L\int_0^Lf\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\end{gathered}\)

Odd periodic expansion:

\(\begin{gathered}f_o(x)=\sn b_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\\[.8em]b_n=\dfrac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\end{gathered}\)

Example: odd HRE of a triangular wave

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2kx}L,&0\lt x\lt\dfrac L2\\\dfrac{2k}L(L-x),&\dfrac L2\lt x\lt L\end{cases}\end{gathered}\)

Odd extension gives a sine series. Split the integral at \(L/2\):

\(\begin{aligned}b_n&=\dfrac2L\int_0^{L/2}\dfrac{2kx}L\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\&\quad+\dfrac2L\int_{L/2}^L\dfrac{2k}L(L-x)\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\[.8em]&=\dfrac{8k}{n^2\pi^2}\sin\dfrac{n\pi}2\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{gathered}f_o(x)=\dfrac{8k}{\pi^2}\Big(\sin\dfrac{\pi x}L-\dfrac1{3^2}\sin\dfrac{3\pi x}L+\dfrac1{5^2}\sin\dfrac{5\pi x}L-\cdots\Big)\end{gathered}\)

11.7 Fourier Integral (FI)

Why: non-periodic functions

Step 0: the pulse and its periodic version

\(f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|\lt1\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\)

Make it periodic with period \(2L\), \(L\gt1\):

\(f_L(x)=\begin{cases}0,&-L\lt x\lt-1\\1,&-1\lt x\lt1\\0,&1\lt x\lt L\end{cases}\)
\(f_L(x+2L)=f_L(x)\ \ \forall x,\qquad L\gt1\)

Step 1: write the series

\(f_L(x)=a_0+\sn\Big(a_n\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L+b_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\Big)\)

The note writes \(\cos nx\) here; for period \(2L\) it is \(\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\).

Step 2: use symmetry

\(f_L\) is an even function, so

\(b_n=0\)

Step 3: find \(a_0\)

\(\begin{aligned}a_0&=\dfrac1L\int_0^Lf_L(x)\,dx\\[.7em]&=\dfrac1L\Big[\int_0^11\,dx+\int_1^L0\,dx\Big]\\[.7em]&=\dfrac1L\end{aligned}\)

Step 4: find \(a_n\)

\(\begin{aligned}a_n&=\dfrac2L\int_0^Lf_L(x)\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\[.7em]&=\dfrac2L\int_0^11\cdot\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\[.7em]&=\dfrac2L\left[\dfrac{\sin\frac{n\pi x}L}{\frac{n\pi}L}\right]_0^1\\[.9em]&=\dfrac2{n\pi}\sin\dfrac{n\pi}L\end{aligned}\)

Step 5: put \(\omega_n=\dfrac{n\pi}L\)

\(a_n=\dfrac2L\cdot\dfrac{\sin\omega_n}{\omega_n}\)

Step 6: result

\(f_L(x)=\dfrac1L+\sn\dfrac2{n\pi}\sin\dfrac{n\pi}L\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L\)
Insight. As \(L\to\infty\) the spacing \(\Delta\omega=\omega_{n+1}-\omega_n=\frac\pi L\to0\), so the line spectrum (heights \(\propto\sin\omega/\omega\), the sinc envelope) becomes a continuous curve. See the visual below.

Fourier integral representation

For \(f\) absolutely integrable (\(\ii|f|<\infty\)), with \(L\to\infty\), \(\Delta\omega\to0\), \(\frac1L=\frac{\Delta\omega}\pi\):

\(f(x)=\io\left[A(\omega)\cos\omega x+B(\omega)\sin\omega x\right]d\omega\)
\(\begin{gathered}A(\omega)=\dfrac1\pi\ii f(v)\cos\omega v\,dv\\[.8em]B(\omega)=\dfrac1\pi\ii f(v)\sin\omega v\,dv\end{gathered}\)

At a jump, the integral equals the average of the left and right limits.

Special cases

\(f\) even (Fourier cosine integral):

\(\begin{gathered}B(\omega)=0\\[.8em]f(x)=\io A(\omega)\cos\omega x\,d\omega\\[.8em]A(\omega)=\dfrac2\pi\io f(v)\cos\omega v\,dv\end{gathered}\)

\(f\) odd (Fourier sine integral):

\(\begin{gathered}A(\omega)=0\\[.8em]f(x)=\io B(\omega)\sin\omega x\,d\omega\\[.8em]B(\omega)=\dfrac2\pi\io f(v)\sin\omega v\,dv\end{gathered}\)

Example: the pulse

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|\lt1\\0,&|x|\gt1\end{cases}\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{aligned}A(\omega)&=\dfrac1\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\cos\omega v\,dv\\[.8em]&=\dfrac{2\sin\omega}{\pi\omega}\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{gathered}B(\omega)=0\quad(f\text{ even})\\[.8em]f(x)=\dfrac2\pi\io\dfrac{\sin\omega\cos\omega x}\omega\,d\omega\end{gathered}\)

The integral equals the average of the limits at a jump, so

\(\begin{gathered}\io\dfrac{\cos\omega x\sin\omega}\omega\,d\omega=\begin{cases}\pi/2,&|x|\lt1\\\pi/4,&|x|=1\\0,&|x|\gt1\end{cases}\end{gathered}\)

Put \(x=0\):

\(\begin{gathered}\io\dfrac{\sin\omega}\omega\,d\omega=\dfrac\pi2\end{gathered}\)

11.8 Fourier Cosine and Sine Transforms

Writing \(A=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\fh_c\) (even \(f\)) or \(B=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\fh_s\) (odd \(f\)) splits the FI symmetrically into a transform and its inverse.

\(\begin{gathered}\fh_c(\omega)=\Fc(f)=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\io f(v)\cos\omega v\,dv\\[.8em]f(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\io\fh_c(\omega)\cos\omega x\,d\omega\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{gathered}\fh_s(\omega)=\Fs(f)=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\io f(v)\sin\omega v\,dv\\[.8em]f(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\io\fh_s(\omega)\sin\omega x\,d\omega\end{gathered}\)

Example

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}k,&0\lt x\lt a\\0,&x\gt a\end{cases}\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{aligned}\fh_c(\omega)&=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\int_0^ak\cos\omega v\,dv\\[.8em]&=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\,k\,\dfrac{\sin a\omega}\omega\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{aligned}\fh_s(\omega)&=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\int_0^ak\sin\omega v\,dv\\[.8em]&=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\,\dfrac k\omega(1-\cos a\omega)\end{aligned}\)

\(\fh_c\) is a sinc function \(\big(\frac{\sin x}x\big)\).

Linearity

\(\Fc(af+bg)=a\Fc(f)+b\Fc(g)\) and \(\Fs(af+bg)=a\Fs(f)+b\Fs(g)\).

Transforms of derivatives

Given \(|f(v)|\to0\) as \(v\to\infty\):

\(\begin{gathered}\Fc(f')=\omega\Fs(f)-\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}f(0)\\[.8em]\Fs(f')=-\omega\Fc(f)\end{gathered}\)

Example: \(\Fc(e^{-ax})\), \(a\gt0\)

\(\begin{gathered}f=e^{-ax}\\[.8em]f'=-ae^{-ax}\\[.8em]f''=a^2e^{-ax}\\[.8em]\Fc(f'')=a^2\Fc(f)\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{aligned}\Fc(f'')&=\omega\Fs(f')-\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}f'(0)\\[.8em]&=-\omega^2\Fc(f)+\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\,a\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{gathered}(a^2+\omega^2)\Fc(f)=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\,a\\[.8em]\Fc(e^{-ax})=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\,\dfrac a{a^2+\omega^2}\end{gathered}\)

11.9 Fourier Transform (FT), Discrete FT (DFT), Fast FT (FFT)

Complex form of the Fourier integral

\(f(x)=\dfrac1{2\pi}\ii\ii f(v)\,e^{i\omega(x-v)}dv\,d\omega\)

The integrand is even in \(\omega\) for the cosine part, so \((0,\infty)\) becomes \((-\infty,\infty)\) with factor \(\frac12\); the sine part is odd in \(\omega\) and integrates to \(0\), so adding \(i\) times it changes nothing.

Fourier transform and inverse

\(\begin{gathered}\fh(\omega)=\FT(f)=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\ii f(x)e^{-i\omega x}dx\\[.8em]f(x)=\FT^{-1}(\fh)=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\ii\fh(\omega)e^{i\omega x}d\omega\end{gathered}\)

Example (a): pulse

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|\lt1\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{aligned}\FT(f)&=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-i\omega x}dx\\[.8em]&=\dfrac{2i\sin\omega}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,i\omega}\\[.8em]&=\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}\,\dfrac{\sin\omega}\omega\end{aligned}\)

Example (b): \(e^{-ax}\), \(a\gt0\)

\(\begin{gathered}f(x)=\begin{cases}e^{-ax},&x\gt0\\0,&x\lt0\end{cases}\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{aligned}\FT(f)&=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^\infty e^{-(a+i\omega)x}dx\\[.8em]&=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\,(a+i\omega)}\end{aligned}\)

Example (c): \(xe^{-x^2}\)

\(\begin{gathered}xe^{-x^2}=-\dfrac12\dfrac d{dx}\big(e^{-x^2}\big)\\[.8em]\FT(e^{-x^2})=\dfrac{e^{-\omega^2/4}}{\sqrt2}\\[.8em]\FT(xe^{-x^2})=-\dfrac{i\omega}2\,\FT(e^{-x^2})=\dfrac{-i\omega\,e^{-\omega^2/4}}{2\sqrt2}\end{gathered}\)

Properties

Linearity:

\(\begin{gathered}\FT(af+bg)=a\FT(f)+b\FT(g)\end{gathered}\)

Derivative (\(f\to0\) at \(\pm\infty\)):

\(\begin{gathered}\FT(f')=i\omega\FT(f)\end{gathered}\)

Convolution:

\(\begin{gathered}(f*g)(x)=\ii f(p)g(x-p)\,dp\\[.8em]\FT(f*g)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\FT(f)\FT(g)\end{gathered}\)

Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)

Take \(f\) as \(2\pi\)-periodic, sample at \(x_k=\frac{2\pi k}N\), \(k=0,\dots,N-1\) (\(N\) samples on \([0,2\pi]\)), and find the complex trigonometric polynomial \(q(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}c_ne^{inx}\) that interpolates \(f\): \(q(x_k)=f(x_k)=f_k\) (curve passes through every point, error \(0\); unlike regression).

\(\begin{gathered}c_m=\dfrac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f_ke^{-imx_k}\\[.8em]\hat f_n=Nc_n=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f_ke^{-inx_k}\\[.8em]\hat f_n=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f_kw^{nk}\\[.8em]w=e^{-2\pi i/N}\end{gathered}\)

In matrix form (an \(A\mathbf x=\mathbf b\) system):

\(\begin{gathered}\hat{\mathbf f}=F_N\mathbf f\\[.8em](F_N)_{nk}=w^{nk}\\[.8em]F_N\overline{F_N}=NI\\[.8em]F_N^{-1}=\tfrac1N\overline{F_N}\end{gathered}\)

Example: DFT of [0, 1, 4, 9]

\(\begin{gathered}\mathbf f=[0,1,4,9]^T,\quad N=4\\[.8em]w=e^{-i\pi/2}=-i\\[.8em]F_4=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&1\\1&-i&-1&i\\1&-1&1&-1\\1&i&-1&-i\end{pmatrix}\\[.8em]\hat{\mathbf f}=F_4\mathbf f=[14,\ -4+8i,\ -6,\ -4-8i]^T\end{gathered}\)

Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)

If \(N=2m\) (even), split \(\mathbf f\) into even-indexed and odd-indexed samples; \(w_N^2=e^{-2\pi i/m}\) is the \(m\)-point root. Compute two DFTs of size \(m\): \(\hat f_{ev}=F_mf_{ev}\), \(\hat f_{odd}=F_mf_{odd}\), then combine, for \(n=0,\dots,m-1\):

\(\begin{gathered}\hat f_n=\hat f_{ev,n}+w_N^n\hat f_{odd,n}\\[.8em]\hat f_{n+m}=\hat f_{ev,n}-w_N^n\hat f_{odd,n}\\[.8em]n=0,\dots,m-1\end{gathered}\)

Example: FFT of [0, 1, 4, 9]

\(\begin{gathered}f_{ev}=[0,4],\quad f_{odd}=[1,9]\\[.8em]F_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad w_4=-i\\[.8em]\hat f_{ev}=F_2f_{ev}=[4,-4]\\[.8em]\hat f_{odd}=F_2f_{odd}=[10,-8]\end{gathered}\)
\(\begin{gathered}\hat f_0=4+10=14\\[.8em]\hat f_1=-4+(-i)(-8)=-4+8i\\[.8em]\hat f_2=4-10=-6\\[.8em]\hat f_3=-4-(-i)(-8)=-4-8i\end{gathered}\)
Insight. Repeating the splitting cuts the cost from about \(N^2\) to about \(N\log_2N\) operations.
