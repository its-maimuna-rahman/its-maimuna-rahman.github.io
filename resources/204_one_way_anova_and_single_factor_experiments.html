Big Picture

Single-Factor Experiment & Completely Randomized Design (CRD)

A single-factor experiment studies the effect of one factor (with several levels) on a response. Other influences that are not of interest are nuisance factors. If experimental units are essentially homogeneous and are assigned to treatments completely at random (no blocking, matching or grouping), the design is a CRD, the simplest design.

Plasma etching example. A wafer is a thin slice of semiconductor; unwanted coating is removed by etching. An engineer studies RF power (160, 180, 200, 220 W) and etch rate (Å/min), with 5 wafers per level: \(a=4,\ n=5,\ N=20\) runs. The 20 runs must be in random order (e.g. assign RAND() to each run and sort). Why: if all 160 W runs came first, a warm-up effect (lower readings the longer the tool is on) would contaminate the data and destroy validity.

Models

Means model: \(y_{ij}=\mu_i+\varepsilon_{ij}\), \(i=1..a,\ j=1..n\). Effects model (one-way ANOVA model): \(y_{ij}=\mu+\tau_i+\varepsilon_{ij}\), with \(\mu_i=\mu+\tau_i\). Here \(\mu\) = overall mean, \(\tau_i\) = effect of treatment \(i\) (how far its mean is from the overall mean), \(\varepsilon_{ij}\) = random error.

Assumption: \(\varepsilon_{ij}\) are independent, normal, mean 0, common variance \(\sigma^2\) (NID\((0,\sigma^2)\)). Hence \(y_{ij}\sim N(\mu+\tau_i,\sigma^2)\). Objective: estimate treatment means and test differences among them.

Fixed vs Random Factor

Fixed effects model

The \(a\) treatments are chosen deliberately by the experimenter. Conclusions apply only to those levels.

Example: doses 5, 10, 20 mg chosen by the researcher.

Random effects model (components of variance model)

The \(a\) treatments are a random sample from a larger population of treatments. Conclusions extend to the whole population; we study variability among treatments.

Example: 10 hospitals randomly selected from hundreds.

All analysis below is for the fixed effects model.

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

Notation

\[ y_{i\cdot}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{ij},\quad \bar y_{i\cdot}=\frac{y_{i\cdot}}{n},\quad y_{\cdot\cdot}=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{ij},\quad \bar y_{\cdot\cdot}=\frac{y_{\cdot\cdot}}{N},\quad N=an \]

Hypotheses

\[ H_0:\mu_1=\dots=\mu_a \ \ (\Leftrightarrow\ \tau_1=\dots=\tau_a=0)\qquad H_1:\mu_i\neq\mu_j\ \text{for at least one pair}\ (\Leftrightarrow \tau_i\ne0 \text{ for some } i) \]

Because \(\mu=\frac1a\sum\mu_i\), the effects satisfy \(\sum_{i=1}^{a}\tau_i=0\). If all means are equal, every deviation from the overall mean is zero.

Tomato example. Means 18, 20, 23, 19 give \(\mu=20\); effects \(-2,0,+3,-1\), which sum to 0.

Partition of variability

\[ \underbrace{\sum_{i}\sum_{j}(y_{ij}-\bar y_{\cdot\cdot})^2}_{SS_T}=\underbrace{n\sum_{i}(\bar y_{i\cdot}-\bar y_{\cdot\cdot})^2}_{SS_{Tr}\ \text{(between)}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i}\sum_{j}(y_{ij}-\bar y_{i\cdot})^2}_{SS_E\ \text{(within)}} \]

The cross-product term vanishes because \(\sum_j(y_{ij}-\bar y_{i\cdot})=0\) (see Derivations). Degrees of freedom: \(SS_T:\ N-1\); \(SS_{Tr}:\ a-1\); \(SS_E:\ a(n-1)=N-a\).

Mean squares: \(MS_{Tr}=SS_{Tr}/(a-1)\), \(MS_E=SS_E/(N-a)\). Two estimates of \(\sigma^2\): one from within treatments, one from between. If means are equal they are similar; a big gap suggests some mean differs.

\[ E(MS_E)=\sigma^2,\qquad E(MS_{Tr})=\sigma^2+\frac{n\sum_i\tau_i^2}{a-1} \]

So under \(H_0\) both estimate \(\sigma^2\); otherwise \(MS_{Tr}\) is larger.

Distribution and test

\(SS_E/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{N-a}\); under \(H_0\), \(SS_{Tr}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{a-1}\) and \(SS_T/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{N-1}\). By Cochran's theorem (df add up: \(\nu=\nu_1+\dots+\nu_s\)), \(SS_{Tr}\) and \(SS_E\) are independent. Hence

\[ F_0=\frac{SS_{Tr}/(a-1)}{SS_E/(N-a)}=\frac{MS_{Tr}}{MS_E}\sim F_{a-1,\,N-a}\ \text{under }H_0;\qquad \text{reject } H_0 \text{ if } F_0>F_{\alpha,\,a-1,\,N-a} \]
Note: \(SS_T/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{N-1}\) : Strictly this holds only under \(H_0\); otherwise it is non-central.

Coding the data

Subtracting a constant (e.g. 600) from every observation does not change any sum of squares (coded totals \(-244,-63,127,535\); grand total 355; same \(SS_T,SS_{Tr},SS_E\)).

Unbalanced data

If treatment \(i\) has \(n_i\) observations, \(N=\sum n_i\) and ANOVA still works with modified formulas:

\[ SS_T=\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}y_{ij}^2-\frac{y_{\cdot\cdot}^2}{N},\qquad SS_{Tr}=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\frac{y_{i\cdot}^2}{n_i}-\frac{y_{\cdot\cdot}^2}{N} \]

Practice (from slides): use the calculator for the unequal-replicate table (A,B,C,D) and the tumor-drug data; for the blood-pressure exercise use \(SS_{Tr}=n\sum(\bar y_i-\bar y_{\cdot\cdot})^2\) with \(\bar y_{\cdot\cdot}=2.55\), \(SS_E=SS_T-SS_{Tr}\).

Estimation of Model Parameters

Least squares minimises \(L=\sum_i\sum_j(y_{ij}-\mu-\tau_i)^2\). Setting \(\partial L/\partial\mu=0\) and \(\partial L/\partial\tau_i=0\) gives \(a+1\) normal equations:

\[ N\hat\mu+n\hat\tau_1+\dots+n\hat\tau_a=y_{\cdot\cdot},\qquad n\hat\mu+n\hat\tau_i=y_{i\cdot}\ \ (i=1..a) \]

The last \(a\) equations add up to the first, so they are not linearly independent and have no unique solution. We impose \(\sum\hat\tau_i=0\) (effects are deviations from the overall mean):

\[ \hat\mu=\bar y_{\cdot\cdot},\qquad \hat\tau_i=\bar y_{i\cdot}-\bar y_{\cdot\cdot},\qquad \hat\mu_i=\hat\mu+\hat\tau_i=\bar y_{i\cdot} \]
Etch: \(\hat\mu=12355/20=617.75\); \(\hat\tau=(-66.55,\,-30.35,\,+7.65,\,+89.25)\). At 160 W the mean etch rate is 66.55 below the overall mean, at 180 W 30.35 below, at 200 W 7.65 above, at 220 W 89.25 above.

Confidence Intervals

Since \(\bar y_{i\cdot}\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma^2/n)\) and \(\sigma^2\) is unknown, use \(MS_E\) and the t distribution:

\[ \bar y_{i\cdot}\pm t_{\alpha/2,\,N-a}\sqrt{\frac{MS_E}{n}},\qquad (\bar y_{i\cdot}-\bar y_{j\cdot})\pm t_{\alpha/2,\,N-a}\sqrt{\frac{2MS_E}{n}} \]

Differences matter because we want to know which treatments differ and by how much.

Etch, 220 W: \(707.00\pm2.120\sqrt{333.70/5}=707\pm17.32\Rightarrow 689.68\le\mu_4\le724.32\).

Simultaneous confidence intervals

A usual \(100(1-\alpha)\%\) CI is one-at-a-time: \(P(\text{this interval is correct})=1-\alpha\). With several intervals, the chance that all are correct is smaller. Bonferroni inequality: \(P(A_1\cup\dots\cup A_r)\le\sum P(A_i)\), so

\[ P(\text{all } r \text{ CIs correct})\ge 1-r\alpha \]

Sampling Distributions

The population distribution is the probability distribution of population values. A sampling distribution is the distribution of a statistic (\(\bar x,\hat p,S^2\)) over all possible samples.

Five midterm scores: 70, 78, 80, 80, 95 (A..E). Distribution: \(P(70)=P(78)=P(95)=0.2,\ P(80)=0.4\). Samples of size 3 without replacement: \(\binom53=\frac{5!}{3!\,2!}=10\). Sampling distribution of \(\bar x\):

Enumerating all samples is usually impossible, so we use known theoretical distributions. Sample mean from a normal population: \(\bar x\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\) exactly; from a non-normal population it is approximately so for large \(n\) (central limit theorem).

Chi-square, t and F

Chi-square \((\chi^2)\): \(Z_1..Z_n\) independent standard normal \(\Rightarrow\chi^2=\sum Z_i^2\) has \(n\) df. For a normal sample, \(\dfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi^2_{n-1}\).

Student's t: \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), \(U\sim\chi^2_n\) independent \(\Rightarrow T=Z/\sqrt{U/n}\sim t_n\). Substituting gives \(t=\dfrac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}\).

F: \(U_1\sim\chi^2_{n_1},U_2\sim\chi^2_{n_2}\) independent \(\Rightarrow F=\dfrac{U_1/n_1}{U_2/n_2}\sim F_{n_1,n_2}\). For two normal samples \(F=\dfrac{S_1^2/\sigma_1^2}{S_2^2/\sigma_2^2}\sim F_{n_1-1,n_2-1}\); if \(\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2\), \(F=S_1^2/S_2^2\) (the variance ratio).

Why in DOE: they give CIs and tests (t, \(\chi^2\), F); F is the foundation of ANOVA.

Hypothesis Testing

A hypothesis is a statement about one or more populations. The null hypothesis \(H_0\) is assumed true until declared false; the alternative \(H_1\) is true if \(H_0\) is false. Like a court case, there are four outcomes:

\(H_0\) true\(H_0\) false
Reject \(H_0\)Type I error, prob. \(\alpha\) (significance level)Correct (power \(=1-\beta\))
Do not rejectCorrectType II error, prob. \(\beta\)

Tails: two-tailed (\(H_1:\mu\ne\mu_0\), key word changed), left-tailed (\(H_1:\mu<\mu_0\), less than), right-tailed (\(H_1:\mu>\mu_0\), higher than). For a left-tailed test \(H_0\) may be written \(\mu\ge\mu_0\).

Test of \(\mu\) when \(\sigma\) is known

\[ \bar x\sim N\!\left(\mu,\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)\ \Rightarrow\ Z=\frac{\bar x-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt n} \]

(Case I: normal population; Case II: non-normal with large \(n\).) Critical values at \(\alpha=0.05\): \(\pm1.96\) (two-tailed), \(+1.65\) (right), \(-1.65\) (left).

Spending: \(H_0:\mu=500\), \(\bar x=470\), \(n=10\), \(\sigma^2=4000\ (\sigma=63.25)\): \(Z=\dfrac{470-500}{63.25/\sqrt{10}}=-1.50\). Two-tailed: inside \(\pm1.96\); right-tailed: below \(1.65\); left-tailed: \(-1.50>-1.65\). In all three, fail to reject \(H_0\).

Model Adequacy Checking

ANOVA is exact if errors are NID\((0,\sigma^2)\). We check this using residuals: \(e_{ij}=y_{ij}-\hat y_{ij}\), where \(\hat y_{ij}=\hat\mu+\hat\tau_i=\bar y_{i\cdot}\). So \(e_{ij}=y_{ij}-\bar y_{i\cdot}\): deviation from its treatment mean.

Normality: histogram and normal probability plot

Residuals should look like a normal sample centred at 0; on a normal probability plot the points follow a straight line. Steps: (1) compute residuals; (2) sort \(e_{(1)}\le\dots\le e_{(n)}\); (3) \(p_i=\dfrac{i-0.5}{n}\) (e.g. \(p_1=2.5\%,\ p_{20}=97.5\%\)); (4) plot \(e_{(i)}\) against \(z_i=\Phi^{-1}(p_i)\) (e.g. \(p=0.05\Rightarrow z\approx-1.64\)).

Etch data: approximately normal; slight curvature at both ends means somewhat thinner tails, not substantial. The calculator draws this plot and a residual table for any data.

Residuals vs run order (independence)

  • Random scatter: independence and constant spread reasonable (etch data: no violation).
  • Long runs: positive autocorrelation. Alternating: negative autocorrelation. Both violate independence.
  • Trend: time drift (tool wear, temperature). Cyclic: periodic source (shifts, batches).
  • Changing spread: non-constant variance.

Residuals vs fitted values

Checks constant variance and model adequacy; residuals should be structureless and unrelated to \(\hat y\) (etch: no unusual structure).

  • Fan / reverse fan: heteroscedasticity. Curvature: missing quadratic term or transformation. Systematic trend: wrong functional form. Clusters: missing factor.

Bartlett's test for equal variances

\(H_0:\sigma_1^2=\dots=\sigma_a^2\) vs \(H_1\): not all equal. For independent samples from normal populations:

\[ \chi_0^2=2.3026\,\frac qc\ \sim\ \chi^2_{a-1}\ (\text{approx.}),\quad q=(N-a)\log_{10}S_p^2-\sum_i(n_i-1)\log_{10}S_i^2 \] \[ c=1+\frac{1}{3(a-1)}\left[\sum_i\frac1{n_i-1}-\frac1{N-a}\right],\qquad S_p^2=\frac{\sum_i(n_i-1)S_i^2}{N-a} \]

Reject \(H_0\) if \(\chi_0^2>\chi^2_{\alpha,a-1}\). (\(2.3026=\ln 10\) converts \(\log_{10}\) to natural log.)

Etch: \(S^2=400.7,280.3,421.3,232.5\); \(S_p^2=333.7\); \(q=0.21\); \(c=1.10\); \(\chi_0^2=2.3026(0.21/1.10)=0.43<\chi^2_{0.05,3}=7.81\). Do not reject: no evidence that the variances differ, agreeing with the residual-vs-fitted plot.
Note: Bartlett's test is sensitive to non-normality; the slide's "no evidence that all four variances are not the same" is a double negative, meaning no evidence of unequal variances.
