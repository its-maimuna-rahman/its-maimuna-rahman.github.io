Demography · Census and Survey Methods

De Facto, De Jure & the Census Pipeline

A census does not simply count people. It first decides which people belong to a place, then moves every household's answer through ten stages before it becomes a published number. This guide takes one apartment in Dhaka and one house in Ohio through that entire pipeline, and shows where the counting rules and the processing machinery decide what the final total turns out to be.

1 · Two doorsteps, one counting rule

On census night, two enumerators are working. One climbs the stairs of a six-storey apartment block in Mirpur, Dhaka. The other rings a doorbell in a suburb of Columbus, Ohio. Both households are ordinary. Both present a counting problem that the census rules must resolve before any number is written down.

The Mirpur household. Five people live in the flat: a married couple, two children, and the husband's mother. Tonight, the eldest son is not there — he works in a garment factory in Chattogram and stays in a rented room there for most of the month, but his name is on the Dhaka rent agreement and he returns every few weeks. Also present tonight is a cousin from Comilla, visiting for a week.
The Columbus household. Two parents and a nineteen-year-old daughter. The daughter is at university in another state and lives in a dormitory nine months of the year; tonight she is on campus, not at home.
The counting rule decides the answer. Under the de facto method, you count everyone present in the dwelling on census night. Under the de jure method, you count everyone whose usual residence is that dwelling, regardless of where they are tonight.

What each country does. The United States counts by usual residence — a de jure rule. College students are counted at their campus address, military personnel at their base, and people with two homes at the one they occupy most of the year. Bangladesh's census also asks about usual residence, but the enumeration is built around who is present in the dwelling when the enumerator arrives, so the published totals behave like a de facto count, with adjustments made for people who move between districts.
The same household, two different totals. If the Mirpur flat reports 6 people under the de facto rule and 6 under the de jure rule — but a different six — the national total can differ sharply once millions of such cases are added up. In a country with heavy internal labour migration, the gap between the two methods is not a rounding error. It is the difference between counting a city's daytime population and its resident population.
The choice of method is made before any enumerator leaves the office. Everything downstream — the questionnaire, the training manual, the edit rules, the published tables — inherits that choice. This is why the ten-step pipeline below begins with defining the concepts, not with counting.
The numbers at stake Bangladesh's 2022 Census produced a preliminary total of 165.16 million, later adjusted to about 170 million after the post-enumeration check. The U.S. 2020 Census counted 331.45 million. Both figures rest on a definition of who belongs where, agreed long before enumeration began.

2 · The two counting rules

De facto method
Every person is counted where they are physically present at the moment of enumeration, regardless of where they normally live. Fast to apply, needs no address register, and suits populations that move frequently. The risk is that people who are away — migrant workers, students, patients — are counted somewhere else, or missed entirely if they are travelling.
De jure method
Every person is counted at their usual place of residence, regardless of where they are at the moment of enumeration. Gives a stable resident population and pairs well with an address list. The risk is that it requires a clear, agreed rule for what "usual" means, and that people with two homes or no fixed address fall through the definition.
Why almost no country uses either rule in its pure form A strict de facto census would count a hotel guest as a resident of the hotel. A strict de jure census would need every household to know exactly which address is "usual". So countries adopt a modified version: they fix a reference moment, write a short list of special cases (students, soldiers, prisoners, diplomats, people in hospitals, seasonal workers), and publish those rules. The rulebook, not the label, is what determines the count.

3 · What the choice actually changes

The de facto and de jure rules give the same answer for most households, because most people sleep where they live. The differences are concentrated in a small set of mobile groups, and those groups are not randomly distributed. They cluster in cities with in-migration, in university towns, in areas with military bases or large construction sites, and along borders.

Three consequences follow, and they matter more than the definitional nicety:

  1. The national total can differ by millions. In a country with tens of millions of internal migrants, changing the residence rule shifts people between districts without changing how many people exist.
  2. Small areas are affected disproportionately. A district that exports workers looks larger under de jure than under de facto; a district that imports them looks the opposite. Planning funds, electoral boundaries and school places are allocated on these small-area totals.
  3. Comparability across rounds breaks if the rule changes. A census that switches methods between rounds produces a spurious jump or drop that looks like demographic change but is not.

The pipeline in one paragraph

A census or a large survey moves through ten linked stages. First the concepts are defined. Then a statistical office is organised and legally mandated to carry out the work. Then the operation is planned and administered — budget, staff, timetable, logistics. Quality control is built in at every stage, not added at the end. Geography turns the country into countable units. Instruments turn the concepts into questions. The enumeration itself collects the answers. Processing converts them into a clean, coded dataset. Review checks that the results make sense. Dissemination puts them in front of users. A failure at any one stage shows up as an error in the final number, and it is usually impossible to tell after the fact which stage caused it.

5 · Steps 1–5: before anyone leaves the office

1Definition of concepts

Every number in a census is the answer to a question, and every question rests on a definition. Before enumeration, the statistical office fixes exactly what counts as a household, who counts as its head, what "urban" means, what counts as literacy, what counts as work, and how age is recorded.

Why definitions decide totals. A household is usually defined as people who live together and share food from a common kitchen. Under that rule a live-in domestic worker may be part of the household; under a rule based only on shared address, they may be a separate household. The same dwelling, two different household counts, and therefore two different average household sizes.
Comparability. Definitions are aligned with international standards so that the country's data can be compared with others. Urban and rural are notorious here: one country's "urban" means a municipality above a population threshold, another's means any settlement with a paved road. Cross-country comparisons of urbanisation can differ by tens of percentage points purely because of definition.
Bangladesh and the United States. Both publish a detailed concepts and definitions manual alongside the census results. The Bangladesh 2022 Census report defines household, head of household, disability and economic activity in separate annexes. The U.S. Census Bureau publishes a residence rules document before every decennial census, listing exactly how students, prisoners, military personnel and people with multiple homes are to be treated.

2Organisation of national statistical offices

Someone has to be legally responsible, funded, and staffed. That someone is the national statistical office, and the way it is organised determines what it can do.

Bangladesh The Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS), under the Ministry of Planning, is the main producer of official statistics. Its legal mandate comes from the Statistics Act 2013. But statistics are also produced by other bodies: the National Institute of Population Research and Training (NIPORT) runs the Demographic and Health Survey; the Directorate General of Health Services produces health statistics; the Office of the Registrar General runs civil registration through BDRIS. This is a decentralised system, and coordinating it is a standing challenge.
United States The U.S. is even more decentralised. The Census Bureau (Department of Commerce) runs the decennial census and the ACS. The National Center for Health Statistics (CDC) runs vital statistics. The Bureau of Labor Statistics runs the Current Population Survey. Each has its own mandate, budget line and independence, which protects against political interference but complicates cross-agency consistency.
Independence and budget. Statistical offices need a legal guarantee that their methods and release dates cannot be changed for political convenience, and a multi-year budget because a census is planned over five to ten years. A census that is funded only in the year it is conducted cannot be planned properly.

3Administration and planning

This is the stage where a statistical exercise becomes a logistical operation. It covers the budget, the timetable, the staffing plan, procurement, legal instruments, pilot tests, cartographic preparation, publicity, and coordination with local government.

Scale in Bangladesh. The 2022 Census employed roughly 370,000 enumerators and supervisors, used digital tablets for the first time, and ran its main enumeration over about ten days in June. The budget was in the order of $140 million. Every one of those tablets had to be procured, configured, charged, distributed, insured and returned.
Scale in the United States. The 2020 Census cost about $13.6 billion, employed around 500,000 temporary workers at peak, and offered online, mail, telephone and in-person response. The cost per person counted was roughly $41, against about $0.85 in Bangladesh.
Pilots and pretests. No questionnaire goes into the field without being tested on a small sample. A pretest reveals that a question is misunderstood, that a form takes too long, or that the tablet software crashes when the battery drops below a threshold. Fixing these before the main operation is far cheaper than fixing them after.
Publicity. Households cooperate when they know the census is legitimate. Bangladesh runs television and mosque announcements; the U.S. runs a paid advertising campaign targeted at hard-to-count communities. A census that nobody has heard of produces refusals and locked doors.

4Quality control

Quality control is not a final inspection. It is a set of checks built into every stage, designed to catch errors while they can still be corrected.

Standardised training. Every enumerator is trained from the same manual, using the same worked examples, and assessed before deployment. Variation in training becomes variation in the data.
Supervision. Enumerators are grouped under supervisors who accompany them on a sample of visits and re-check a sample of completed questionnaires. The ratio of enumerators to supervisors is a planning decision with direct consequences for data quality.
Re-interviews and spot checks. A random sample of households is revisited by a supervisor and asked the same key questions. Discrepancies flag a problem with a particular enumerator, not with the population.
Real-time monitoring. Where enumeration is done on tablets, each submission carries a timestamp, a GPS location and an interview duration. A cluster of interviews completed in three minutes, or all recorded at the same GPS point, is a strong signal of fabrication and can be investigated while the team is still in the field.
Post-enumeration survey. After the census, an independent sample survey re-enumerates a small number of areas. Comparing the two counts estimates how many people were missed or double-counted. Bangladesh ran such a check after the 2022 Census, which is why the preliminary total of 165.16 million was adjusted to about 170 million.

5Geography

A country is too large to count as a single unit, so it is divided into pieces small enough for one enumerator to cover in the available time. These pieces are the enumeration areas, and they are drawn on maps before enumeration begins.

Enumeration areas and household listing. Each enumeration area is designed to contain a manageable number of households — typically 100 to 150 for a single enumerator in a ten-day census. Before the main count, enumerators walk the area and list every structure and household, so that they know what they are supposed to find and can be checked against it later.
Bangladesh The 2022 Census used a geospatial enumeration architecture, with enumeration areas drawn on digital maps and tablets carrying the area boundaries. This was a major change from earlier paper-based censuses and made it possible to monitor coverage area by area.
United States The Census Bureau maintains TIGER, a digital map of every street, and divides the country into census tracts, block groups and blocks. An address canvassing operation walks the country in the year before the census to update the address list, since the U.S. census is largely mail- and internet-based.
Geography is also the sample frame. Surveys do not draw households from thin air. They draw primary sampling units from the census geography, then list households within them, then select. A poor map produces a poor sample.

6 · Steps 6–7: the instrument, and the field

6Census instruments

The instrument is the questionnaire plus everything that supports it: the enumerator manual, the training guide, the translation, the software, and the show-cards used in the field.

One concept per question. "Do you work, and if so what is your occupation and income?" is three questions fused into one, and will produce unusable answers. Each item on a census form asks about one thing, in language the respondent can interpret without training.
Short form versus long form. A census asks a short set of questions of everyone (age, sex, relationship, residence) and a longer set of a sample. The U.S. decennial census is short-form only; the detailed questions moved to the ACS. Bangladesh's census schedule is longer, covering housing, amenities, disability and economic activity for every household.
Translation and back-translation. A questionnaire written in one language and used in another must be translated, then independently translated back, and the two versions compared. Otherwise a question about "work" may be understood as paid employment in one version and any income-generating activity in another.
Paper versus digital. Bangladesh's 2022 Census used computer-assisted personal interviewing on tablets, with built-in range checks and skip logic. The U.S. offers online, paper, telephone and in-person modes. Each mode has its own error profile: paper forms need manual data entry and allow skipped questions; digital forms can prevent some errors but can also prevent an enumerator from recording an unusual but true answer.

7Conducting the census and survey

This is the stage where the operation meets reality. A household is not available, a lane is flooded, a respondent misunderstands, a tablet runs out of charge. The quality of the entire census depends on how well the field plan anticipated these situations.

Who is at home, and who answers

Timing of visits. Enumerators work in the evening and at weekends when adults are at home, and return at a different time of day to a household that was locked on the first visit. A single daytime visit in a working-class neighbourhood will systematically miss employed adults — a bias, not a random error.
Respondent rules. The head of household answers for everyone, which is efficient but introduces proxy reporting: a husband reporting his wife's age, education and work status. Proxy answers are systematically less accurate for some items, particularly age of women, income and disability. Sensitive items are best asked of the person themselves, and many surveys arrange for female enumerators to interview women in private.
Households with nobody at home. At least three revisit attempts, spread across different days and times, before a household is recorded as absent. The number of attempts is a planning decision and must be documented, because "absent" households are a coverage loss that cannot be recovered later.

Sampling for surveys: what changes when you are not counting everyone

A census counts everyone. A survey counts a sample and multiplies up. The design of that sample is where surveys succeed or fail.

The frame. Households are selected from a list. For a national survey, the frame is built from census enumeration areas: a sample of areas is chosen, then households are listed within each chosen area, then a sample of households is drawn from the list. This is multistage sampling, and it is the standard design behind Bangladesh's SVRS, BDHS and the U.S. ACS.
Stratification. The country is divided into strata — urban and rural, or by division, or by region — and a sample is drawn from each. Stratification guarantees that every part of the country is represented and reduces the variance of the national estimate.
Clustering and the design effect. Because interviewing is expensive, surveys cluster households in a limited number of areas. Households in the same village resemble each other more than households drawn at random, so the effective sample size is smaller than the number of interviews. The ratio is the design effect, usually between 1.5 and 2.0 for clustered household surveys. A sample of 10,000 with a design effect of 1.8 behaves like a simple random sample of about 5,500.
Weights. Each household is selected with a known probability, so each receives a weight equal to the inverse of that probability. Weights are then adjusted for non-response and calibrated to census totals by age and sex. Without weights, a survey over-samples the easily reached and under-represents everyone else.

Practical checks that protect a survey in the field

Season and calendar. Avoid Ramadan and Eid, the monsoon flood season, harvest peaks and school examination weeks. A survey fielded during a flood measures the flood, not the population.

Consent and confidentiality. Explain who is conducting the survey, what the data will be used for, and that individual answers will not be shared with any other agency. Cooperation depends on this being true, not just stated.

Length of interview. A household interview beyond about 45 to 60 minutes produces fatigue and rushed answers. Long instruments should be split or shortened.

Recall period. Questions about events must specify a fixed window — "in the last 12 months" — and the window must be consistent across the instrument. Vague recall periods produce inflated counts of recent events.

Non-response follow-up. Refusals are converted by a second, more experienced visit. Unconverted refusals are recorded and their number reported, because a low response rate is a quality statement about the survey, not a footnote.

Field editing. The supervisor checks each completed questionnaire for completeness and internal consistency before it leaves the area. Corrections made at the doorstep are far cheaper than corrections made at the processing centre.

8Processing the data

Processing turns a stack of forms — or a stream of tablet uploads — into a clean, coded dataset. It is where most silent errors are introduced or removed.

Capture. Paper forms are scanned or keyed; digital forms upload to a central server. Every record receives a unique identifier and is linked to its enumeration area, so that coverage can be checked geographically.
Coding. Free-text answers — occupation, industry, cause of death, place of birth — are converted into standard codes. Coding rules must be written down and applied consistently, because a coder who interprets "garment worker" as manufacturing in one district and as trade in another creates an artificial regional difference.
Editing. Range checks (age cannot exceed 120), consistency checks (a five-year-old cannot be married), and structural checks (every household must have a head) are run automatically. Failing records are referred back to the original form or, if that is impossible, flagged.
Imputation. Missing or impossible values are filled in using rules — typically by copying the value from a similar record (hot-deck imputation). Imputation is unavoidable in a large census; the essential discipline is to count and report how many values were imputed, so users can judge the effect.
Weighting and tabulation. Survey records receive their design weights, which are adjusted for non-response and post-stratified to census totals. The weighted file is then tabulated into the published tables.
Bangladesh and United States Bangladesh's 2022 Census processed tablet submissions centrally, with automatic checks at the point of entry and a data-processing unit at BBS. The U.S. Census Bureau runs a large automated processing system with documented edit and imputation rules for both the decennial census and the ACS.

9Data review

Before publication, the results are examined for plausibility. Review does not usually change the count, but it decides whether the count is published as-is, adjusted, or accompanied by a warning.

Internal consistency. Sex ratios by age, household size distributions, and the ratio of children to women of childbearing age are checked against expectation. A sudden excess of people at ages ending in 0 or 5 is age heaping — a sign that respondents are rounding their ages, and a clue about data quality.
Comparison with previous rounds. A district that grew by 40% in ten years is either a genuine boom town or an enumeration problem. Demographers use the cohort component method to project what the population should be, given births, deaths and migration, and compare the projection with the count.
Post-enumeration survey. An independent re-count of a sample of areas estimates net coverage error. Bangladesh's post-enumeration check after the 2022 Census led to an upward adjustment of about five million people. The U.S. runs a similar Post-Enumeration Survey, and publishes separate estimates for groups that are at risk of being undercounted.
Decision to adjust. Whether to publish the raw count or an adjusted figure is a policy decision, taken transparently and documented. Either way, the unadjusted count and the adjustment must both be reported, or users cannot reconcile the numbers.

10Dissemination

Data that nobody can find, or nobody can understand, has not been collected. Dissemination is the final step and the one most often under-resourced.

Sequence of releases. A preliminary total is released within weeks — Bangladesh's 2022 preliminary report appeared on 27 July 2022, about a month after enumeration. Detailed national tables follow, then district and sub-district reports, then microdata.
Formats. PDF reports for narrative and reference; spreadsheet and database files for analysis; interactive portals for non-specialists. Bangladesh publishes through the BBS website and the national open data portal. The U.S. publishes through data.census.gov and the CDC's WONDER system.
Confidentiality. Small-area tables are checked for disclosure risk. A table that identifies a single household — the only widow in a small village, for example — is suppressed or aggregated before publication. This is a legal obligation in both countries.
User consultation. Users are asked in advance what tables they need. A census that publishes what the statisticians found interesting, rather than what planners and researchers need, wastes a large part of its value.

7 · Why the method and the pipeline matter

Bangladesh
De facto counting suits a mobile population. With tens of millions of internal migrants — garment workers, construction labourers, rickshaw pullers, seasonal agricultural workers — a strict de jure rule would require every household to state a single usual address with confidence. Counting people where they are found is more robust, provided the results are adjusted for the districts that export and import workers.
United States
De jure counting suits a stable address system. A mail-based census requires an address list, and a de jure rule supplies one. The cost is that people without a stable address — renters who move often, undocumented immigrants, people displaced by hurricanes or wildfires — are the most likely to be missed, which is why the Census Bureau publishes separate coverage estimates for these groups.
Bangladesh
The pipeline is where coverage is won or lost. The gap between the preliminary 165.16 million and the adjusted 170 million is roughly the size of a major city. It is not a counting error in the ordinary sense; it is what a post-enumeration check is for.
United States
The pipeline is where money is allocated. Census totals determine the apportionment of the House of Representatives and guide the distribution of roughly $1.5 trillion a year in federal funds. A coverage error of a fraction of a percent is a budget decision worth billions.
The common lesson Both countries face the same structural problem: the people most in need of being counted are the hardest to count. The de facto and de jure rules differ in which groups they miss. The ten-step pipeline exists to make those misses visible, measurable, and where possible, correctable.

10 · Derivations and worked numbers

Derivation 1 · How the two residence rules produce different totals

Let \(P\) be the number of people whose usual residence is a given area, \(A\) the number of those residents who are temporarily away, and \(V\) the number of visitors present who usually live elsewhere. Then

\[\text{De jure count}=P,\qquad \text{De facto count}=P-A+V.\]

The difference is \(V-A\), the net temporary presence. Summed over all areas, the national de facto total equals the national de jure total, because every visitor is an absentee somewhere else. The rules change the distribution, not the national total — unless people are missed or double-counted at the margin.

Worked example. A district has \(P=1{,}200{,}000\) usual residents; 80,000 of them work in the capital for most of the year; 30,000 people from other districts are staying there temporarily. Then

\[\text{De jure}=1{,}200{,}000,\qquad \text{De facto}=1{,}200{,}000-80{,}000+30{,}000=1{,}150{,}000.\]

A 50,000-person gap in one district — about 4% — is enough to change school-place allocations, road budgets and constituency boundaries.

Derivation 2 · Sample size for a survey proportion

For a simple random sample, the sample size needed to estimate a proportion \(p\) with margin of error \(e\) at confidence level \(z\) is

\[n_0=\frac{z^2\,p(1-p)}{e^2}.\]

Clustered designs inflate this by the design effect \(\text{DEFF}\), and a finite population correction may reduce it:

\[n=\frac{n_0\times\text{DEFF}}{1+\dfrac{n_0\times\text{DEFF}-1}{N}}.\]

Worked example. Take \(p=0.5\), \(e=0.02\), \(z=1.96\):

\[n_0=\frac{1.96^2\times0.25}{0.02^2}=\frac{0.9604}{0.0004}=2{,}401.\]

With a design effect of 1.8, \(n=2{,}401\times1.8=4{,}322\) households. For a national survey of 10,000 households, the effective sample is \(10{,}000/1.8\approx5{,}556\) — the clustering costs about 44% of the nominal sample.

Derivation 3 · Non-response bias

Suppose a survey achieves a response rate \(r\), and the responding group has mean \(\bar y_r\) while non-respondents have mean \(\bar y_n\). The true mean is

\[\bar y=r\,\bar y_r+(1-r)\,\bar y_n,\]

so the bias from using the respondents alone is

\[\text{Bias}=\bar y_r-\bar y=(1-r)(\bar y_r-\bar y_n).\]

Worked example. A survey of labour force participation achieves \(r=0.70\). Respondents report a participation rate of 0.62; non-respondents, who tend to be out at work all day, would have reported 0.78. Then

\[\bar y=0.70(0.62)+0.30(0.78)=0.434+0.234=0.668,\qquad \text{Bias}=0.62-0.668=-0.048.\]

A 4.8 percentage-point understatement of labour force participation, produced entirely by who was at home. Revisit attempts and evening interviews are the standard defence.

Derivation 4 · Estimating coverage from a post-enumeration survey

Let \(C\) be the number of people found by the census, \(S\) the number found by an independent post-enumeration survey in the same areas, and \(M\) the number found by both. The dual-system estimate of the true population is

\[\hat T=\frac{C\times S}{M}.\]

Worked example. In the sampled areas, the census counts 48,000 people, the survey counts 50,000, and 45,000 are found in both:

\[\hat T=\frac{48{,}000\times50{,}000}{45{,}000}=53{,}333.\]

Census coverage is \(48{,}000/53{,}333\approx90\%\), so the census missed about 10% of the population in those areas. Scaling that coverage rate to the national count gives the adjusted total. The method assumes the two sources are independent, which is never exactly true — people missed by both tend to be the most mobile — so the estimate is a lower bound on the true undercount.

Derivation 5 · Weighting a sample back to the population

A household selected with probability \(\pi_i\) receives a base weight \(w_i=1/\pi_i\). Weights are adjusted for non-response within weighting classes, then calibrated so that the weighted sample matches known population totals \(N_g\) in group \(g\):

\[w_i^{\text{cal}}=w_i^{\text{nr}}\times\frac{N_g}{\sum_{j\in g}w_j^{\text{nr}}}.\]

Worked example. A stratum contains 120,000 women aged 20–29 in the population, but the 1,500 responding women in that stratum carry a combined non-response-adjusted weight of only 100,000. The calibration factor is \(120{,}000/100{,}000=1.2\), applied to every respondent in the stratum. Without this step, the survey would understate young women by 20,000 in every table it publishes.
