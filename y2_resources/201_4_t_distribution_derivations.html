Student’s t Distribution: Derivations from First Principles

This guide derives every property of \(t_\nu\): the density, the raw moments, the moment generating function (MGF), the cumulant generating function (CGF), the characteristic function (CF), the mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis and mode. One shortcut does most of the work. Write \(T\) as a normal variable divided by an independent scaled chi-squared variable, then freeze the chi-squared part at a fixed number. Given that number, \(T\) is just a normal variable, and the rest is averaging over the chi-squared part.

1 · Tools used throughout

1.1 The gamma and beta functions

For \(s\gt 0\), \(\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty u^{\,s-1}e^{-u}\,du\).

Recurrence. Integrate by parts with \(u^{s}\) and \(e^{-u}\): \(\Gamma(s+1)=\big[-u^{s}e^{-u}\big]_0^\infty+s\int_0^\infty u^{s-1}e^{-u}du=s\,\Gamma(s)\).
Value at one half. Put \(u=y^2\): \(\Gamma(\tfrac12)=\int_0^\infty u^{-1/2}e^{-u}du=2\int_0^\infty e^{-y^2}dy=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-y^2}dy=\sqrt\pi\).
Scaled gamma integral. For \(c\gt0\), substituting \(u=cv\): \[ \int_0^\infty v^{\,s-1}e^{-cv}\,dv=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{c^{\,s}} . \tag{G} \]
Beta function. \(B(p,q)=\int_0^1 u^{p-1}(1-u)^{q-1}du=\dfrac{\Gamma(p)\Gamma(q)}{\Gamma(p+q)}\). Substituting \(u=r/(1+r)\), so that \(1-u=1/(1+r)\) and \(du=dr/(1+r)^2\), turns it into \[ \int_0^\infty \frac{r^{\,p-1}}{(1+r)^{\,p+q}}\,dr=B(p,q). \tag{B} \]

1.2 The standard normal

Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\). Complete the square in the exponent, \(sz-\tfrac{z^2}{2}=-\tfrac{(z-s)^2}{2}+\tfrac{s^2}{2}\), to get

\[ E\!\left[e^{sZ}\right]=e^{s^2/2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(z-s)^2/2}dz=e^{s^2/2}. \]

Expand both sides in powers of \(s\). The left side is \(\sum_n E[Z^n]s^n/n!\) and the right side is \(e^{s^2/2}=\sum_m s^{2m}/(2^m m!)\). Matching coefficients:

\[ E[Z^{2m+1}]=0,\qquad E[Z^{2m}]=\frac{(2m)!}{2^m\,m!}=1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2m-1)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(2j-1). \tag{N} \]

The same calculation with \(s=i\sigma t\) shows that \(N(0,\sigma^2)\) has characteristic function \(e^{-\sigma^2t^2/2}\).

1.3 The chi-squared variable

\(V\sim\chi^2_\nu\) has density \(f_V(v)=\dfrac{v^{\nu/2-1}e^{-v/2}}{2^{\nu/2}\Gamma(\nu/2)}\) for \(v\gt0\). For any real \(r\gt-\nu/2\), apply (G) with \(s=\nu/2+r\) and \(c=\tfrac12\):

\[ E\!\left[V^{\,r}\right]=\frac{1}{2^{\nu/2}\Gamma(\nu/2)}\int_0^\infty v^{\frac\nu2+r-1}e^{-v/2}dv=2^{\,r}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2+r\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)} . \tag{V} \]

The condition \(r\gt-\nu/2\) is exactly what keeps \(s\) positive. Negative powers of \(V\) therefore have finite expectation only when the power is small enough, and this one fact controls which moments of \(t_\nu\) exist.

1.4 Generating functions

\(M_T(t)=E[e^{tT}]\), \(K_T(t)=\ln M_T(t)\) and \(\varphi_T(t)=E[e^{itT}]\). When \(M_T\) is finite near 0, \(E[T^n]=M_T^{(n)}(0)\) and \(\kappa_n=K_T^{(n)}(0)\).

2 · Where \(t_\nu\) comes from

Take \(n\) independent \(N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) values with sample mean \(\bar X\) and sample variance \(S^2\). Three facts hold: \(\bar X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\); \(V=(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}\); and \(\bar X\), \(S^2\) are independent. Divide the top and bottom of \(\dfrac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\) by \(\sigma/\sqrt n\):

\[ \frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}=\frac{\sqrt n(\bar X-\mu)/\sigma}{S/\sigma}=\frac{Z}{\sqrt{V/(n-1)}},\qquad Z=\frac{\sqrt n(\bar X-\mu)}{\sigma}\sim N(0,1). \]

The unknown \(\sigma\) cancels. This motivates the definition used below.

Definition Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) and \(V\sim\chi^2_\nu\) be independent. Then \[ T=\frac{Z}{\sqrt{V/\nu}}\sim t_\nu . \] The sample-mean statistic above is \(t_{n-1}\).

3 · Deriving the density

The idea. Fix \(V=v\). Then \(T=Z\sqrt{\nu/v}\) is a normal variable multiplied by a constant, so it is normal. Write down its density, then average over all values of \(v\).

Step 1: the density given \(V=v\). A constant \(c\) times \(Z\) has variance \(c^2\). Here \(c^2=\nu/v\), so \(T\mid V=v\sim N(0,\nu/v)\) with \[ f(x\mid v)=\sqrt{\frac{v}{2\pi\nu}}\;e^{-vx^2/(2\nu)} . \]
Step 2: average over \(v\). \[ f(x)=\int_0^\infty f(x\mid v)\,f_V(v)\,dv . \]
Step 3: combine powers and exponentials. The powers of \(v\) are \(v^{1/2}\cdot v^{\nu/2-1}=v^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}-1}\). The exponents add to \(-\tfrac v2-\tfrac{vx^2}{2\nu}=-\tfrac v2\left(1+\tfrac{x^2}{\nu}\right)\). So \[ f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}\;2^{\nu/2}\,\Gamma(\nu/2)}\int_0^\infty v^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}-1}\,e^{-\frac v2\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)}dv . \]
Step 4: apply (G) with \(s=\frac{\nu+1}{2}\) and \(c=\frac12\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)\): \[ \int_0^\infty v^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}-1}e^{-cv}dv=\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right)\,2^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}} . \]
Step 5: collect the constants. \[ \frac{2^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\nu}\;2^{\nu/2}}=\frac{2^{1/2}}{\sqrt2\,\sqrt{\pi\nu}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\nu}} . \]
\[ f_\nu(x)=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\;\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)}\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}},\qquad -\infty\lt x\lt\infty . \]

3.1 Check that it integrates to one

The density is even, so \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}dx=2\int_0^\infty(\cdots)dx\). Substitute \(x=\sqrt{\nu s}\), so \(dx=\dfrac{\sqrt\nu}{2\sqrt s}ds\):

\[ 2\int_0^\infty(1+s)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\frac{\sqrt\nu}{2\sqrt s}\,ds=\sqrt\nu\int_0^\infty\frac{s^{\frac12-1}}{(1+s)^{\frac12+\frac\nu2}}\,ds=\sqrt\nu\;B\!\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac\nu2\right) \]

by (B) with \(p=\tfrac12\), \(q=\tfrac\nu2\). Multiply by the constant in front of the density:

\[ \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)}\cdot\sqrt\nu\cdot\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac12\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac12)}{\sqrt\pi}=1 . \]

3.2 The case \(\nu=1\)

With \(\Gamma(1)=1\) and \(\Gamma(\frac12)=\sqrt\pi\), the constant is \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt\pi\sqrt\pi}=\dfrac1\pi\), so \(f_1(x)=\dfrac{1}{\pi(1+x^2)}\). This is the Cauchy density. It has no mean, as Section 4 confirms.

4 · Raw moments

4.1 The one idea

Write \(T=Z\cdot W\) with \(W=\sqrt{\nu/V}\). \(Z\) and \(W\) are independent, so the expectation of a product is the product of expectations:

\[ E[T^n]=E[Z^n]\;E[W^n]=E[Z^n]\;\nu^{n/2}\,E\!\left[V^{-n/2}\right]. \]

4.2 The normal part

From (N): \(E[Z^n]=0\) for odd \(n\), and \(E[Z^{2m}]=\prod_{j=1}^m(2j-1)\).

4.3 The chi-squared part

Apply (V) with \(r=-n/2\). It requires \(-\tfrac n2\gt-\tfrac\nu2\), that is \(n\lt\nu\):

\[ E\!\left[V^{-n/2}\right]=2^{-n/2}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu-n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)},\qquad n\lt\nu . \]

For even \(n=2m\), apply \(\Gamma(s)=(s-1)\Gamma(s-1)\) repeatedly, \(m\) times, starting from \(s=\nu/2\):

\[ \Gamma\!\left(\tfrac\nu2\right)=\left(\tfrac\nu2-1\right)\left(\tfrac\nu2-2\right)\cdots\left(\tfrac\nu2-m\right)\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac\nu2-m\right) . \]

Therefore \(\dfrac{\Gamma(\nu/2-m)}{\Gamma(\nu/2)}=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{\nu/2-j}=2^m\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{\nu-2j}\), and the powers of 2 cancel:

\[ E\!\left[V^{-m}\right]=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\nu-2j},\qquad E[W^{2m}]=\nu^m\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{\nu-2j}. \]

4.4 Combine

\[ E[T^{2m}]=\nu^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{2j-1}{\nu-2j}\quad(2m\lt\nu),\qquad E[T^{2m+1}]=0\quad(2m+1\lt\nu). \]

An equivalent form uses \(\prod_{j=1}^m(2j-1)\cdot2^{-m}=\Gamma(m+\tfrac12)/\Gamma(\tfrac12)\):

\[ E[T^{2m}]=\nu^{m}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac12\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2-m\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac12\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)} . \]

4.6 Why moments stop existing

The condition \(n\lt\nu\) can be seen in the density as well. For large \(|x|\), \(f_\nu(x)\) behaves like a constant times \(|x|^{-(\nu+1)}\). Then \(\int^\infty x^n\,x^{-(\nu+1)}dx\) converges exactly when \(n-\nu-1\lt-1\), that is \(n\lt\nu\). When \(n\ge\nu\) and \(n\) is even, the moment is infinite. When \(n\ge\nu\) and \(n\) is odd, the positive and negative sides are both infinite, so the moment is undefined.

5 · The moment generating function

Result \(M_T(t)=\infty\) for every \(t\ne0\). The MGF of \(t_\nu\) does not exist.
Step 1: lower bound on the density. For \(|x|\ge\sqrt\nu\) we have \(1+x^2/\nu\le 2x^2/\nu\). The exponent \(-\tfrac{\nu+1}{2}\) is negative, so the inequality flips: \[ f_\nu(x)\ \ge\ C\,|x|^{-(\nu+1)},\qquad C=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac\nu2\right)}\left(\frac{2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\gt0 . \]
Step 2: the exponential beats any power. For \(t\gt0\) and every integer \(m\), \(e^{tx}\ge(tx)^m/m!\) when \(x\gt0\). Choose \(m\gt\nu\). Then \[ \int_{\sqrt\nu}^\infty e^{tx}f_\nu(x)\,dx\ \ge\ \frac{C\,t^m}{m!}\int_{\sqrt\nu}^\infty x^{\,m-\nu-1}dx=\infty , \] because \(m-\nu-1\gt-1\). The same argument on the negative side handles \(t\lt0\).

So no series in \(t\) built from the MGF is available, and the moments of Section 4 had to be found another way. Only at \(t=0\) is \(M_T(0)=1\). The characteristic function in Section 7 replaces the MGF.

6 · The cumulant generating function

\(K_T(t)=\ln M_T(t)\) is undefined for \(t\ne0\), since \(M_T\) is infinite there. Individual cumulants can still be defined through the moments that exist, using the relations between cumulants and central moments (for a variable with mean 0, \(\kappa_2=\mu_2\), \(\kappa_3=\mu_3\), \(\kappa_4=\mu_4-3\mu_2^2\)). Because the mean is 0, central and raw moments coincide.

\(\kappa_2\). \(\kappa_2=\dfrac{\nu}{\nu-2}\), for \(\nu\gt2\).
\(\kappa_3\). \(\kappa_3=E[T^3]=0\), for \(\nu\gt3\).
\(\kappa_4\). \[ \kappa_4=\frac{3\nu^2}{(\nu-2)(\nu-4)}-3\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}\right)^2=\frac{3\nu^2\left[(\nu-2)-(\nu-4)\right]}{(\nu-2)^2(\nu-4)}=\frac{6\nu^2}{(\nu-2)^2(\nu-4)},\qquad\nu\gt4 . \]

All odd cumulants vanish by symmetry, and cumulants of order \(r\) exist only when \(r\lt\nu\).

7 · The characteristic function

\(\varphi_T(t)=E[e^{itT}]\) exists for every distribution. The freeze-and-average idea works again.

Step 1: freeze \(V=v\). Given \(V=v\), \(T\sim N(0,\nu/v)\), whose characteristic function is \(e^{-\frac{\nu}{v}\cdot\frac{t^2}{2}}\) by Section 1.2.
Step 2: average over \(v\). \[ \varphi_T(t)=E\!\left[e^{-\nu t^2/(2V)}\right]=\frac{1}{2^{\nu/2}\Gamma(\nu/2)}\int_0^\infty v^{\frac\nu2-1}\,e^{-\frac v2-\frac{\nu t^2}{2v}}\,dv . \]
Step 3: match a named integral. The modified Bessel function \(K_s\) of the second kind is defined by \[ K_s(z)=\frac12\int_0^\infty u^{\,s-1}e^{-\frac z2\left(u+\frac1u\right)}du,\qquad z\gt0 . \] Put \(c=\sqrt\nu\,|t|\) and substitute \(v=cu\). Then \(\frac v2=\frac{cu}{2}\) and \(\frac{\nu t^2}{2v}=\frac{c^2}{2cu}=\frac{c}{2u}\), so the exponent is \(-\frac c2\left(u+\frac1u\right)\). Also \(v^{\frac\nu2-1}dv=c^{\nu/2}u^{\frac\nu2-1}du\). The integral equals \(c^{\nu/2}\cdot2K_{\nu/2}(c)\).
\[ \varphi_T(t)=\frac{\left(\sqrt\nu\,|t|\right)^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\!\left(\sqrt\nu\,|t|\right)}{2^{\nu/2-1}\,\Gamma(\nu/2)},\qquad\varphi_T(0)=1 . \]

7.1 Check: \(\nu=1\)

\(K_{1/2}(z)=\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{2z}}\,e^{-z}\). With \(z=|t|\), \(\Gamma(\frac12)=\sqrt\pi\) and \(2^{\nu/2-1}=2^{-1/2}\):

\[ \varphi_T(t)=\frac{|t|^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2|t|}}\,e^{-|t|}}{2^{-1/2}\sqrt\pi}=e^{-|t|}, \]

the known characteristic function of the Cauchy distribution. Its sharp corner at \(t=0\) reflects the missing mean.

7.2 Check: the variance from a small-\(t\) expansion

Use \(e^{-y}\approx1-y\) in \(\varphi_T(t)=E[e^{-\nu t^2/(2V)}]\):

\[ \varphi_T(t)\approx1-\frac{\nu t^2}{2}\,E\!\left[\frac1V\right]=1-\frac{\nu}{2(\nu-2)}\,t^2 , \]

using \(E[1/V]=\dfrac{1}{\nu-2}\) from Section 4.3. A distribution with mean 0 and variance \(\sigma^2\) has \(\varphi(t)\approx1-\sigma^2t^2/2\), so \(\sigma^2=\dfrac{\nu}{\nu-2}\). This requires \(E[1/V]\lt\infty\), that is \(\nu\gt2\), the same condition as before.

8 · Mean and variance

Mean

\(E[T]=E[Z]\,E[W]=0\cdot E[W]=0\), provided \(E[W]=\sqrt\nu\,E[V^{-1/2}]\) is finite. By Section 4.3 that needs \(1\lt\nu\). The density is also symmetric about 0, which gives the same answer. For \(\nu\le1\) the mean is undefined.

Variance, route A: from the moments

\[ \operatorname{Var}(T)=E[T^2]-0=1\cdot\nu\cdot E[V^{-1}]=\frac{\nu}{\nu-2},\qquad\nu\gt2 . \]

Variance, route B: split by the frozen value

Given \(V=v\), the variance of \(T\) is \(\nu/v\) and the mean is 0. Taking the average over \(v\): \(E[T^2]=E[\nu/V]=\nu E[1/V]=\nu/(\nu-2)\). For \(1\lt\nu\le2\), \(E[1/V]=\infty\), so the variance is infinite.

Variance, route C: the characteristic function

Section 7.2 gave the same value from the small-\(t\) expansion.

\[ E[T]=0\ (\nu\gt1),\qquad\operatorname{Var}(T)=\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}\ (\nu\gt2). \] The variance exceeds 1 and tends to 1 as \(\nu\to\infty\).

9 · Skewness

The mean is 0, so central and raw moments agree. Skewness is \(\mu_3/\mu_2^{3/2}\).

Third moment. From Section 4, odd moments are 0 whenever they exist: \(E[T^3]=E[Z^3]E[W^3]=0\), valid for \(\nu\gt3\). The density is also an even function, so \(\int x^3f_\nu(x)dx\) cancels between the two sides.
\[ \gamma_1=\frac{0}{\left(\nu/(\nu-2)\right)^{3/2}}=0,\qquad\nu\gt3 . \]

For \(\nu\le3\) the third moment does not exist, so skewness is undefined even though the curve is perfectly symmetric.

10 · Kurtosis

With mean 0, \(\mu_4=E[T^4]\). Use the formula of Section 4.4 with \(m=2\):

\[ E[T^4]=\nu^2\cdot\frac{1\cdot3}{(\nu-2)(\nu-4)}=\frac{3\nu^2}{(\nu-2)(\nu-4)},\qquad\nu\gt4 . \]

Divide by the squared variance:

\[ \beta_2=\frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2}=\frac{3\nu^2}{(\nu-2)(\nu-4)}\cdot\frac{(\nu-2)^2}{\nu^2}=\frac{3(\nu-2)}{\nu-4} . \]

Subtract 3 to get the excess:

\[ \gamma_2=\frac{3(\nu-2)}{\nu-4}-3=\frac{3(\nu-2)-3(\nu-4)}{\nu-4}=\frac{6}{\nu-4} . \]
\[ \beta_2=\frac{3(\nu-2)}{\nu-4},\qquad\gamma_2=\frac{6}{\nu-4},\qquad\nu\gt4 . \] Cross-check: \(\kappa_4/\kappa_2^2=\dfrac{6\nu^2}{(\nu-2)^2(\nu-4)}\cdot\dfrac{(\nu-2)^2}{\nu^2}=\dfrac{6}{\nu-4}\), the same.

The excess is positive, so the tails are heavier than normal. It shrinks like \(6/\nu\), and for \(2\lt\nu\le4\) the fourth moment is infinite.

11 · The mode

Take the log of the density. Only one term depends on \(x\):

\[ \ln f_\nu(x)=\text{const}-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right) . \]

Differentiate:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\ln f_\nu(x)=-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\cdot\frac{2x/\nu}{1+x^2/\nu}=-\frac{(\nu+1)\,x}{\nu+x^2} . \]

The denominator is always positive, so the sign is the sign of \(-x\): the density increases for \(x\lt0\), decreases for \(x\gt0\), and the only stationary point is \(x=0\).

\[ \text{mode}=0\quad\text{for every }\nu\gt0 . \] Mean, median and mode all equal 0 whenever the mean exists.

12 · Large \(\nu\): the normal limit

Why \(T\to Z\). \(V/\nu\) has mean 1 and variance \(2\nu/\nu^2=2/\nu\), which tends to 0. So \(V/\nu\to1\), and \(T=Z/\sqrt{V/\nu}\to Z/1=Z\).

The density. Expand the log of the variable part with \(\ln(1+y)=y-\tfrac{y^2}{2}+\cdots\) and \(y=x^2/\nu\):

\[ -\frac{\nu+1}{2}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)=-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\left(\frac{x^2}{\nu}-\frac{x^4}{2\nu^2}+\cdots\right)\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac{x^2}{2}\quad(\nu\to\infty). \]

So \(\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\to e^{-x^2/2}\). The constant in front must then tend to \(1/\sqrt{2\pi}\), because the density always integrates to 1.

The moments agree. \(\dfrac{\nu}{\nu-2}=1+\dfrac{2}{\nu-2}\to1\) and \(\gamma_2=\dfrac{6}{\nu-4}\to0\), the variance and excess kurtosis of \(N(0,1)\). The sizes of the corrections, \(2/(\nu-2)\) and \(6/(\nu-4)\), measure how far \(t_\nu\) is from normal at a given \(\nu\).
